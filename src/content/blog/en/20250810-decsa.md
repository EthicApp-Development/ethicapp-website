# EthicApp Experience Presented at DECSA 2025 Conference
*August 8th, 2025*

Claudio Álvarez and María José Cornejo presented the paper *“Teaching Ethical Deliberation: Reflective Implementation of EthicApp in Midwifery Education”* at the **XXV Conference on Health Sciences Education**, organized by the Department of Health Sciences Education (DECSA) of the Faculty of Medicine at the University of Chile.

The presentation described an interdisciplinary educational experience developed during the second semester of 2024 in the course *Community Health I*, with first-year midwifery students. The initiative aimed to address persistent challenges in ethics education in health, including the predominance of lecture-based teaching, limited student participation, and insufficient development of ethical argumentation.

To tackle these challenges, the teaching team implemented **EthicApp** as a pedagogical tool to support structured ethical deliberation. Two clinical cases involving ethical dilemmas in primary healthcare—one focused on gender-based violence—were designed to immerse students in realistic decision-making scenarios.

The instructional design followed a five-phase sequence, combining individual reflection, written justification, and anonymous group deliberation through chat. This structure allowed the team to observe how students’ ethical judgments evolved through argumentation, peer interaction, and exposure to diverse perspectives.

The experience was implemented in two in-person sessions and analyzed using a qualitative exploratory-descriptive approach, including classroom observations, reflective teaching logs, and open-ended student feedback.

Results highlighted the pedagogical value of EthicApp in structuring ethical deliberation, fostering active participation—including from typically less vocal students—and supporting the development of critical thinking and ethical reasoning. The use of anonymity and synchronous interaction was particularly effective in creating a safe and respectful space for discussion.

From a teaching perspective, the experience also challenged traditional instructional roles, emphasizing the importance of facilitating rather than directing ethical discussions. It further underscored the need for ongoing development of teaching competencies in ethical facilitation.

The team concluded that EthicApp offers strong potential for integration into health curricula, particularly in supporting ethical decision-making, professional judgment, and values-based education aligned with institutional missions in healthcare training.
