# Introduction to Ethical Artificial Intelligence at Universidad Panamericana
*January 2026*

In January 2026, researchers Claudio Álvarez and Gustavo Zurita delivered a two-session teacher training course on **Ethical Artificial Intelligence** for academics at the Universidad Panamericana, Guadalajara campus.

This initiative emerged from a collaboration with **Yurixhi Gallardo Martínez**, Professor–Researcher at the Institute of Humanities of Universidad Panamericana, whose work has been instrumental in bridging ethical reflection and technological development within higher education.

## Course Overview

The course was designed as an introduction to the conceptual, ethical, and pedagogical foundations of artificial intelligence, with a particular focus on its implications for university teaching.

The first part of the course addressed the evolution and main paradigms of AI, including symbolic AI, connectionism, and probabilistic approaches, along with a discussion of the current landscape of applications such as natural language processing, computer vision, and decision-making systems.

A key conceptual contribution of the course was the distinction between *intelligence* and *agency*, emphasizing that modern AI systems are better understood as systems capable of acting effectively rather than truly “understanding” in a human sense.

## Ethical Foundations

A central component of the training focused on ethical frameworks for AI. Participants explored:

- The convergence of international guidelines (UNESCO, European Union, and others).
- The bioethical principles underlying AI ethics: beneficence, non-maleficence, autonomy, and justice.
- The critical role of **explainability** as a condition for accountability and responsible use of AI.

These discussions were complemented by reflections on current regulatory developments, including the European AI Act and risk-based approaches to governance.

## AI in Higher Education

A substantial portion of the course was devoted to the role of AI in education. The sessions highlighted both opportunities and risks:

**Opportunities:**
- Personalized learning support
- Immediate feedback for students
- Expanded access to educational resources

**Risks:**
- Over-reliance on AI tools
- Reduction in critical thinking and independent reasoning
- Potential biases and misinformation in AI-generated content

These tensions were discussed within a broader vision of education as a fundamentally human process, where AI should augment —not replace— the role of the teacher.

## Generative AI and Prompting in Teaching Practice

The second part of the course focused on **generative AI**, including large language models (LLMs), their probabilistic nature, and their limitations (e.g., hallucinations, bias, and lack of true understanding).

A major emphasis was placed on **prompting as a pedagogical tool**, introducing academics to a range of structured techniques such as:

- In-context learning (ICL)
- Few-shot prompting
- Chain-of-thought reasoning
- Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)
- Plan-and-solve and reflective prompting strategies

These techniques were framed not as technical tricks, but as ways to **support academic judgment, foster critical thinking, and maintain epistemic control** in teaching and research contexts.

## Relevance for Academic Practice

The course addressed a growing need among university faculty: moving beyond superficial or instrumental uses of AI toward **ethically grounded, pedagogically meaningful integration**.

Rather than positioning AI as a replacement for academic work, the training emphasized:

- The centrality of human judgment
- The importance of transparency and responsible use
- The design of learning activities that remain resistant to automation
- The development of students’ ethical and critical capacities

## Looking Forward

This experience at Universidad Panamericana reflects a broader line of work within the EthicApp project and related initiatives, aimed at supporting educators in navigating the complex intersection of **AI, ethics, and higher education**.

As AI technologies continue to evolve, the challenge is not only technological, but fundamentally educational: to ensure that these tools contribute to the formation of reflective, responsible, and critically engaged professionals.
