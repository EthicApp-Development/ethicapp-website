# Paper on IntelligentEthicApp accepted at TECH-EDU 2026
*February 20, 2026*

We are pleased to announce that the paper **“Towards a multi-agent GenAI architecture to support ethics education: A human-centered design process”** has been accepted at the international conference **TECH-EDU 2026**, which will take place from June 22 to 24, 2026 in Alexandroupolis, Greece.

The paper presents recent advances from the **IntelligentEthicApp** project, focused on the design and development of a multi-agent architecture based on generative artificial intelligence to support ethics education in higher education.

## About the paper

The paper proposes an architecture that extends EthicApp—a CSCL platform for learning through ethical dilemmas—by incorporating specialized agents capable of supporting ethical decision-making processes through structured inquiry-based activities.

The work addresses one of the main challenges identified in the use of collaborative tools for ethics education: the tendency of students to quickly converge toward consensus, limiting argumentative diversity and the depth of moral reasoning.

To tackle this issue, the paper proposes the use of generative AI agents such as:

- Mediator agents that intervene in group discussions.
- “Devil’s advocate” agents that introduce counterarguments.
- Argumentation tutors that support the construction of more elaborated justifications.

These components are part of a modular architecture designed to support different stages of the ethical learning process.

## Methodological approach

One of the main contributions of the paper is its **Human-Centered Design (HCD)** approach, where technological development is carried out in close collaboration with educators, researchers, and other relevant stakeholders.

The paper documents a co-design process that extended over more than one year and involved more than 20 participants from different disciplines and educational contexts. This process included stages such as:

- Identification of pedagogical needs.
- Idea generation and prioritization.
- Definition of requirements.
- Architectural design.
- Prototype development.
- Formative evaluation.

This approach made it possible to align technological decisions with real pedagogical needs, while also addressing key aspects such as teacher and student agency in AI-mediated environments.

## Main contributions

Among the most relevant contributions of the paper are:

- The proposal of a multi-agent architecture for ethics learning using generative AI.
- Empirical evidence on the application of human-centered design approaches in AI-based educational systems.
- Identification of key limitations in collaborative ethical discussions, such as premature consensus and low-quality argumentation.
- Design principles and lessons learned that can inform other AI systems in education.

## Outlook

The work presented at TECH-EDU 2026 represents an important step in the development of IntelligentEthicApp, laying the groundwork for integrating these prototypes into the EthicApp platform.

The next stages of the project include broader evaluations with students, iterative refinement of the agents, and the consolidation of a community of practice around the use of artificial intelligence in ethics education.
