# Conference on Hybrid Intelligence at the 10th UNAB Teaching Innovation Conference
*January 9, 2026*

Professor Claudio Álvarez participated as a speaker in the **10th Teaching Innovation Conference: Hybrid Intelligence in Higher Education**, held on January 8–9, 2026, at the Antonio Varas Campus of Universidad Andrés Bello (UNAB).

In this event—focused on reflecting upon the use of artificial intelligence in university teaching—he presented the talk titled **“Hybrid Intelligence for Ethics Education in Engineering: Lessons from EthicApp and the IntelligentEthicApp Project.”**

## About the talk

The presentation addressed the challenge of teaching engineering students to **deliberate and justify ethical decisions in complex socio-technical contexts**, emphasizing the need for pedagogical approaches that integrate both cognitive and social dimensions of moral reasoning.

It also discussed the use of **ethics micro-insertions** as a curricular strategy, enabling the integration of ethical reflection into technical courses without the need to create standalone ethics subjects, thus supporting contextualized and discipline-based learning.

Additionally, **EthicApp** was presented as a CSCL (Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning) infrastructure that enables the design and execution of learning activities based on ethical dilemmas, combining individual and collaborative phases, including group discussion mediated by chat.

## Hybrid intelligence and the future of ethics education

One of the central themes of the talk was the concept of **hybrid intelligence**, understood not as a simple combination of human and artificial intelligence capabilities, but as a distributed system of activity in which human agency is amplified through pedagogically designed technological tools.

In this context, advances from the **IntelligentEthicApp** project were presented, incorporating generative AI-based agents to support processes such as ethical argumentation and the mediation of group discussions.

Preliminary results suggest that these agents can contribute to improving students’ argumentative quality and facilitating discussion dynamics, while also raising challenges related to interaction design, timing of interventions, and clarity of feedback.

## Final reflection

The talk concluded by emphasizing that designing educational experiences with artificial intelligence requires a **human-centered approach**, where the roles and limits of AI must be defined based on pedagogical and ethical criteria, with the aim of fostering meaningful learning in collaborative contexts.
