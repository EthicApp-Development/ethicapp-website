# Article on AI-Based Automatic Content Selection for Teacher Support Published in J.UCS
*November 28th, 2025*

We are pleased to announce the publication of a new research article by **Claudio Álvarez**, **Gustavo Zurita**, and **Andrés Carvallo** — researcher at the National Center for Artificial Intelligence (**CENIA**, Chile) — in the *Journal of Universal Computer Science (J.UCS)*.

The work addresses one of the major challenges of technology-supported collaborative learning environments: the large amount of information generated by students during discussion and deliberation activities, and the difficulty this creates for real-time teacher monitoring and orchestration.

## Automatic content selection for teacher support

The research proposes a method for the **automatic selection of relevant content** aimed at supporting teachers during collaborative learning activities, helping facilitate monitoring and pedagogical decision-making processes in CSCL (*Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning*) environments.

The method seeks to automatically identify relevant students' written justifications, reducing the burden associated with manually reviewing large volumes of textual interaction.

As part of the study, different instances of the method based on various **large language models (LLMs)** were compared, evaluating their ability to identify content that is useful and pedagogically meaningful for instructors.

## Connection with EthicApp and IntelligentEthicApp

The work was developed using data and scenarios associated with **EthicApp**, particularly in collaborative ethical deliberation contexts.

This line of research is directly connected to the pedagogical orchestration challenges present in complex collaborative activities, and contributes to the development of future functionalities within **IntelligentEthicApp**, where AI-based agents are expected to assist teachers through monitoring and facilitation-support tools.

The research adopts a human-centered perspective, understanding that artificial intelligence does not replace teachers’ pedagogical judgment, but rather can function as a support mechanism to improve situational awareness and reduce operational load in collaborative settings.
