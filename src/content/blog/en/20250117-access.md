# Article on Teacher Orchestration Load Published in IEEE
*January 17th, 2025*

We are pleased to announce the publication of a new scientific article by Claudio Álvarez and Gustavo Zurita, in collaboration with researchers from **Universitat Pompeu Fabra (UPF)** in Barcelona, Spain.

The work was carried out together with **Davinia Hernández-Leo**, Director of the **TIDE (Technology Enhanced Knowledge and Interaction Group)** research group, as well as **Ishari Amarasinghe**, postdoctoral researcher at UPF, and **Lubna Hakami**, PhD student at the same institution.

The publication proposes a framework aimed at understanding and measuring **teacher orchestration load** in technology-supported collaborative learning (*Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning*, CSCL) environments.

## What is teacher orchestration load?

In CSCL settings, teachers must simultaneously coordinate multiple processes: monitoring groups, interpreting interactions, managing timing, responding to contingencies, supporting discussions, and keeping learning activities aligned with pedagogical objectives.

This complexity creates a significant cognitive and operational burden for educators facilitating these learning experiences, especially when activities involve multiple groups working in parallel through digital tools.

The concept of **teacher orchestration load** seeks to describe and analyze these demands associated with the design, monitoring, and facilitation of collaborative technology-mediated learning activities.

## Contribution of the framework

The article introduces a conceptual and methodological framework that contributes to characterizing different dimensions of orchestration load, supporting its analysis and potential measurement in authentic classroom settings.

The work aims to provide tools for better understanding how technological and pedagogical design decisions affect the teacher experience, and how future educational platforms may reduce operational friction without sacrificing pedagogical flexibility.

As part of the case study reported in the publication, **EthicApp** was used in collaborative ethical deliberation activities, allowing the researchers to observe concrete challenges related to teacher monitoring, coordination, and facilitation in authentic learning scenarios.

This line of research is particularly relevant for the future development of **IntelligentEthicApp**, where AI-based agents are expected to support pedagogical orchestration processes through a human-centered approach.
