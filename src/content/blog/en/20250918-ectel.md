# Best Poster Award at ECTEL 2025 for Research on Teacher Agency and Orchestration
*September 2025*

The poster *“Towards Capturing Teacher Agency Manifestations within Orchestration Activities: A Conceptual-Analytical Framework”* received the **Best Poster Award** at the **20th European Conference on Technology Enhanced Learning (ECTEL 2025)**, held in Newcastle and Durham, United Kingdom.

The work was presented by **Víctor Alonso-Prieto**, PhD student at the University of Valladolid under the supervision of Prof. Yannis Dimitriadis, and co-authored with Yannis Dimitriadis, Luis P. Prieto, Gustavo Zurita, and Claudio Álvarez.

## About the poster

The poster introduces the **TAMOA framework** (Teacher Agency Manifestations within Orchestration Activities), a conceptual-analytical framework designed to study how teacher agency is expressed in the orchestration of technology-enhanced learning (TEL) activities.

TAMOA integrates established theories of human and teacher agency with models of orchestration in TEL, enabling researchers to identify both observable actions (e.g., adapting a learning activity) and reflective processes (e.g., reasoning behind instructional decisions) through which teachers influence learning environments.

The framework is illustrated through a case study involving the orchestration of an ethics learning activity supported by a collaborative platform, showing how teacher agency manifests across design, management, awareness, and adaptation phases.

## Relevance

This recognition at ECTEL 2025 highlights the relevance of studying **teacher agency** in increasingly complex educational settings shaped by digital and AI-driven technologies.

By providing a structured way to analyze how teachers interact with and adapt technology-supported learning environments, TAMOA contributes to the design of systems that better support educators’ decision-making and professional autonomy.

The work is particularly aligned with ongoing research on **EthicApp** and related platforms, where the orchestration of collaborative ethical discussions requires both technological support and active teacher facilitation.
